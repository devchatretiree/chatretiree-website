About ChatRetiree

Clear answers, and a calm second look, whenever you need one.

ChatRetiree helps you check a message before you act, understand Medicare and Social Security in plain words, keep up with the scams going around, and know what to do if a scam already happened. It works like a knowledgeable friend: it looks at things with you and lets you decide.

Photo by Vitaly Gariev on Unsplash

Why we started

People who have spent a lifetime working, raising families and running households know how to figure things out. What’s changed is the amount of noise. Fake texts, look-alike emails, robocalls and fine print arrive every day, and they get more convincing every year.

Meanwhile, the good answers are out there, spread across long guides and official websites that take time to find and read. One “Is this real?” moment can turn into an afternoon of wondering and worrying.

We built ChatRetiree to make the trustworthy information easy to reach and quick to check, so you can get on with your day.

Our logo is a porch light: someone’s home, and they’ve left the light on for you. Day or night, it’s there when something doesn’t feel right.

Our Promises

What you can count on

  • Official sources only

    Medicare answers come from the official Medicare guides, and Social Security answers from Social Security’s own publications. Scam news comes from the FTC, the FBI and the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services, and help after a scam follows the FTC and IdentityTheft.gov.

  • Plain words

    No jargon and no alarm bells. Just what we found and what we’d do next.

  • Honest about what we don’t know

    We never call anything “safe.” When we can’t tell, or the official sources don’t cover something, we say so.

  • You stay in charge

    We suggest next steps. You decide. Nothing is sent to anyone unless you press send.

  • Your information stays yours

    We don’t keep what you check, we hide private details before anything leaves your phone, and we never sell your data.

  • Your peace of mind comes first

    Everything we suggest is meant to protect you and your money. That’s why we’ll never suggest paying a scammer anything, not even a smaller amount to make them go away.

Where our answers come from

We only use sources you could look up yourself:

  • Medicare.gov, including the official “Medicare & You” handbook and guides
  • Social Security publications from SSA.gov
  • Federal Trade Commission consumer alerts
  • FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center
  • HHS Office of Inspector General
  • IdentityTheft.gov, for the official steps after your information is stolen
  • Google Web Risk, to check links against known harmful sites without opening them

For questions about your own plan or benefits, we point you to the free official help that can look at your situation: 1‑800‑MEDICARE, your local SHIP counselor, or Social Security.
